The Word of Wisdom

20-minute Young Men lesson • Doctrine and Covenants 89; Daniel 1

Big idea: God gives us the Word of Wisdom to help us care for our bodies, develop self-mastery, protect our freedom, and become spiritually receptive.

3 minutes

1. Opening — Why does God care?

“If you could eat nothing but candy, drink soda all day, and stay up until 3 a.m., would you be breaking the Word of Wisdom?”

Let the class debate before offering your own thoughts.

  • Is something automatically good for us because it isn't specifically prohibited?
  • Why would God care about what we eat or drink?
  • What's the difference between obedience and health?
Teaching point: The Word of Wisdom includes specific commandments and broader principles for caring for our bodies. These are related, but not identical.
5 minutes

2. Scripture discovery — What does D&C 89 say?

Ask three class members to read these passages, then discuss.

Doctrine and Covenants 89:4

Why does the Lord warn about people who might take advantage of our weaknesses?

Consider advertising, addictive products, and businesses that profit from repeated consumption.

Doctrine and Covenants 89:10–12

What does the Lord encourage us to consume?

Notice the positive guidance about plants, fruits, and moderation.

Doctrine and Covenants 89:18–21

What blessings are promised?

Health, wisdom, knowledge, strength, and divine protection. These are promises of blessing, not a guarantee that faithful people will never become sick.

Ask: “Which of these verses do Church members talk about the least?”

The Church teaches specific standards regarding alcohol, tobacco, coffee, tea, and harmful drugs, alongside broader principles of wise care for the body.

5 minutes

3. Real-life situations — Freedom or restriction?

Read a situation and let the class decide whether it illustrates wisdom, obedience, self-control, or a combination.

Scenario 1 of 4

Key question: “Does having fewer restrictions always mean having more freedom?”

Someone who develops an addiction may have fewer rules but less control over their choices.

4 minutes

4. Daniel — Courage to be different

Read Daniel 1:8, 15, 17, and 20.

Daniel and his friends were taken into Babylon and faced pressure to adopt the customs of the king's court. Daniel chose not to defile himself with the king's food and drink.

  • Why would refusing the king's food require courage?
  • What would be a modern equivalent of Daniel's situation?
  • Did Daniel receive only physical blessings, or spiritual blessings too?
Insight: Daniel wasn't merely choosing a diet. He was demonstrating loyalty to God when conforming would have been easier.
3 minutes

5. Closing — One personal decision

“If you really believed your body was a gift from God, what is one thing you might do differently this week?”

Invite a few responses without pressuring anyone to share personal struggles. Examples: better sleep, balanced eating, avoiding addictive substances, limiting screen time, or exercising.

“What if one of the greatest blessings of the Word of Wisdom isn't just living longer, but becoming more capable of hearing and following God?”

Close with a brief testimony that Heavenly Father gives commandments because He loves us, and that obedience and wise choices can help us become spiritually stronger.

Extra discussion if you have time

“Why does Doctrine and Covenants 89:4 warn about people who want to profit from our weaknesses?”

Discuss marketing, dependency, self-control, and the difference between making a choice and losing the ability to choose freely.

Takeaway: God's commandments are meant to increase our freedom, not merely restrict behavior.
